## EE: solved by using the right formulation — quantile regression and the ceiling classifier

Following the root-cause analysis, encapsulation efficiency was reformulated
into two questions the data can answer.

**1. Conditional quantile regression (q25 / q50 / q75).** A quantile random
forest (400 trees, grouped folds, median-imputed features) beats the
marginal-quantile baseline at every tested quantile on the full EE set
(pinball loss: q25 7.16 vs 7.93, q50 7.29 vs 7.93, q75 4.58 vs 7.93 pp;
n = 803) and on a ceiling-free interior working subset (EE 40-96, measured during
model development on the pre-clean cohort: q75 3.82 vs 5.82). Quantile predictions are the honest form for a bounded, skewed
literature outcome; all three are reported in the working-archive quantile study and reproduced by the shipped evidence script.

**2. The ceiling classifier.** The question "does a formulation reach
EE >= 95%?" - the top of the commercial specification band - is answered by
a gradient-boosting classifier at **ROC-AUC 0.627** under the same grouped
folds (`results/classification.csv`); ranking on the regression score itself
answers the same question (best PR-AUC 0.346 at the 22.5% base rate,
`results/ceiling_classifier.csv`, regenerated by
`data/generate_derived_tables.py`);
DL regression reaches R² 0.360 for the quantitative outcome.

**3. The DL band classifier (drug loading).** Low <10 / moderate 10-25 /
high >25 percent: balanced accuracy **0.643** (chance 0.333), per-class AUC
0.80-0.87 - the strongest categorical view in the release. DL also keeps its
quantitative regression.

## Size and zeta: meaningful band classification, no mean regression

Both targets have established pharmaceutical band definitions, and both
support modest but real classification under the same grouped folds
(`results/classification.csv`):

| Target | Classes | Balanced accuracy | One-vs-rest AUC |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Particle size | <100 nm (EPR-accessible) / 100-200 nm (long-circulation) / >200 nm (rapid clearance) | 0.378 (chance 0.333) | 0.641 / 0.597 / 0.553 |
| Zeta potential | anionic (<-10 mV) / neutral / cationic (>+10 mV) | 0.388 (chance 0.333) | 0.578 / 0.624 / 0.587 |

Feature-block ablation (run without fold-local column screening, so its
full-model rows differ slightly from `results/classification.csv`): the zeta
signal is carried by the feed-ratio inputs (removing them costs 0.049
balanced accuracy; every other block is neutral or helpful to remove); the
size-band signal is weakly distributed with no single driver. Mean regression for these two targets stays at or below zero
and is not offered.

**The 95% threshold is a reporting-behavior landmark, stated honestly.**
The corpus density rises monotonically toward 100% with no interior valley
at 95 (Hartigan dip test p = 0.73: no distributional bimodality; the shape is
left-skewed with a ceiling pile-up, bimodality coefficient 0.38, below the 5/9 cutoff). Industry anchors
describe commercial specifications of EE >= 90-95% (Hoang et al.,
Pharmaceutics 2026, doi:10.3390/pharmaceutics18030396) and "typically greater
than 80%" (McKenzie et al., Curr Protoc 2023, doi:10.1002/cpz1.874); 95% is
therefore predeclared as the top of the commercial specification band, not
as a discovered distributional mode, and the classifier is accompanied by an
AUC-vs-threshold sensitivity analysis (80/85/90/93/95/97: AUC 0.571-0.632;
shipped evidence script: figures/quantile_and_threshold_evidence.py).

## Why EE point regression fails (root-cause analysis)

Two independent investigations converge on a dual data defect rather than a
modeling failure. (Numbers below were measured on the pre-clean 1,171-row
cohort; the direction of every conclusion is unchanged on the released
1,108-row table.)

1. **Ceiling selection bias.** 23% of corpus EE values sit at >=95% (the
   literature reports successful formulations), a near-constant block with
   SD 1.85. Every positive EE R^2 in any model is partial recognition of this
   "did it hit the ceiling" binary structure; within the learnable 5-95%
   range the best model's R^2 is negative (-0.04).
2. **Label-basis incomparability.** Among the 572 records reporting EE, DL
   and a feed ratio, the DL implied by (EE, ratio) contradicts the reported
   DL by more than 2x for 24.5% of records (7.9% by more than 10x) - i.e.,
   about a quarter of labels are computed on different denominators.
3. **The physical drivers are absent.** The same feed-ratio trunk that gives
   drug loading R^2 = 0.23 gives EE 0.00: EE is governed by drug solubility
   in the organic phase and drug-polymer affinity, for which the corpus has
   no proxy (lipid-composition ratios 5% coverage; polymer MW 0%; HLB 0%).
4. **Reporting noise floor.** Near-identical formulations (same drug,
   carrier, method) report EE differing by 12-13 percentage points on
   average, capping any feature set's achievable R^2 near 0.7 - and only
   cross-drug-transferable signal is usable under grouped folds.
5. **Literature high-R^2 EE results are not transferable.** Single-system
   subsets reach R^2 0.30-0.45 under the random splits the literature uses,
   but drop to <=0 under grouped folds, and the gain survives removal of the
   process features - i.e., it is drug-identity memorization, not physics.

An independent check on a newly collected, healthier-distribution corpus
(570 EE records; median 74, SD 26; process features HLB/solvent/concentration
that the main corpus lacks) reproduces the result: best R^2 = 0.014,
indistinguishable from the mean baseline. See
`analysis/special_studies/` for the full protocols (response materials are
provided separately from this repository).

EE/DL errors are percentage points; size errors nm and zeta errors mV.
TabPFN is the final model in the regression ladder for the two percentage outcomes; the EE quantitative view is the quantile model above. The mean and
Ridge references use the same records and folds as nested selection.

Drug loading shows moderate internal signal; EE gains are small, and zeta
and size gains are limited. Unavailable chemical structures share one group:
152 DL, 161 EE, 131 zeta and 223 size records. This does not establish
publication independence or prospective new-material performance. Outcome-based
screening limits these results to the selected cohort; source values remain unverified.

`oof_predictions.csv` supplies labeled outer predictions; `fold_allocations.csv`,
`inner_oof_predictions.csv`, `inner_scores.csv` and `selected_feature_columns.csv`
support assessment of fold isolation and selection. Figures are computed from
these files and the classification protocol (see `figures/`). No
carrier-ranking or universal predictability claim is made.

## Full-text retrieval notes

The extraction harness consumes full-text XML from the Europe PMC open-access
endpoint (verified working). Article PDFs and embedded figure images are
additionally attempted (`literature.fetch_pdf`,
`literature.extract_figures_from_xml`) so vision-capable endpoints can read
tables and figures; several content CDNs refuse programmatic PDF/image access
(403/404), in which case the manifest records the failure and extraction
proceeds from the XML text. This is a network-policy limitation, not a
pipeline defect.
